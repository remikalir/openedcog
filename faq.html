Frequently asked questions

Short answers to the questions families ask most. For step-by-step coaching, see the parent guide. For why this project exists, see About.

About OpenedCog

What is OpenedCog?

OpenedCog is a free website where young children can practice puzzles in the same formats as the CogAT (Cognitive Abilities Test) at Level 8, the level most schools give in second grade. OpenedCog covers all nine subtests, reads every question aloud, and has a learning mode with explanations and a pretend-test-day mode that works like the real thing. A Grown-ups page shows you progress for your child or children.

Is it really free? What's the catch?

There's no catch. OpenedCog has no ads, no accounts, no upsells, and no data collection. The code and content of OpenedCog are openly licensed, so you can check how it works or make your own copy of this project.

Who made it?

Remi Kalir, an educator and parent, built OpenedCog in collaboration with Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic. It began as practice for his own child's screening, and he made it public so other families could use it too. The About page shares his story in greater detail.

Is OpenedCog connected to the test publisher or to my school?

No. OpenedCog is independent. It isn't affiliated with or endorsed by Riverside Insights, which publishes the CogAT, or by any school or district. CogAT® is a registered trademark of Riverside Assessments, LLC.

Are these real CogAT questions?

No. Every puzzle is original, written to match the publicly described format of each subtest. Some are hand-written and some are generated fresh each time. None are real test items.

What practice can and can't do

Will practicing raise my child's score?

OpenedCog cannot promise that, and you should be wary of any service or technology that claims puzzle practice will raise your child's score. The CogAT measures reasoning, which isn't something a child memorizes. What practice can do is remove surprise: the voice, the buttons, and what each question type is asking. A child who has never seen a puzzle like this may lose points to confusion rather than reasoning. Familiarity helps the score reflect the child.

Do OpenedCog scores predict my child's CogAT results?

No. OpenedCog practice scores are on original puzzles and don't convert to CogAT percentiles, scores, or placement. Use them only to decide what to practice next.

Is it fair to prepare for an ability test?

Families disagree, and so do educators. Here's the reasoning behind OpenedCog: test-prep businesses already sell CogAT practice, so children whose families can pay often walk in familiar with the format while other children see it cold. A free, honest resource narrows that gap a little. It's built for familiarity, not drilling. Some schools discourage outside preparation, so you may want to ask yours.

How much should my child practice?

About 15 to 25 minutes a session. If the test is two weeks away, about ten sessions is plenty. If it's further off, two short sessions a week will do. More practice won't build reasoning, and too much can make a child dread the test. The sample plan in the guide spells it out.

Is it right for my child?

My child isn't in second grade. Can they use it?

OpenedCog follows Level 8, usually given in second grade. Kindergarten and first-grade levels (5/6 and 7) use mostly the same picture formats with easier items, so younger children can try it with a grown-up nearby. One format differs: their Number Puzzles look different. From third grade (Level 9), the CogAT adds reading, so OpenedCog doesn't match those levels. The code is open on GitHub and you are welcome to adapt it for another level.

Our district uses the CogAT Screening Form. Does OpenedCog still help?

Yes. The Screening Form uses just three subtests: Picture Analogies, Number Analogies, and Figure Matrices. In OpenedCog, those are the Picture pairs, Number pairs, and Shape pairs practice tiles. If your child moves on to the full test, the other six tiles cover the rest.

My child's school gives the test on paper. Is this still useful?

Yes. The puzzle types are the same on paper, where a teacher usually reads the questions aloud. OpenedCog models the online version, so just skip the parts about buttons.

Is OpenedCog available in Spanish?

Not yet. A Spanish version would need a translated interface and verbal puzzles written for Spanish speakers, not just translated. If you'd like to help, see How can I help? below. The online CogAT itself offers Spanish audio at this level, so ask your school whether your child can take it in Spanish.

Can children with disabilities use OpenedCog?

Partly, and we want to be honest about the limits. Everything around the puzzles is built for keyboards and screen readers: the buttons, menus, and Grown-ups page. Picture puzzles announce their pictures by name. But the number, shape, and paper-folding puzzles are visual reasoning tasks by design, so a screen reader can't fully convey them. The real test can't either. If your child has an IEP or 504 plan, ask the school which accommodations apply during testing.

Using OpenedCog

What devices and browsers work?

Phones, tablets, Chromebooks, and laptops, in any up-to-date browser. Read-aloud works best in Chrome, Safari, and Edge. For practice, use the same kind of device your child will test on if you can. There's nothing to install.

The read-aloud voice isn't working.

Make sure the device isn't muted. On an iPhone or iPad, check the silent switch too. Then open Grown-ups, check that Read-aloud voice is On, and tap Test the voice. If it's still silent, try Chrome, Safari, or Edge. The voice comes from your browser, so it sounds different on different devices.

Can more than one child use it on the same device?

Yes. Tap Add another child under the greeting on the home screen. Each child gets their own history and settings, and you switch between them from the home screen.

Does it need the internet?

Yes, to load the site and its pictures. There's no account to sign in to and nothing to download.

Why is there a math question on the Grown-ups button?

A simple gate that keeps young children out of the settings. It's a different multiplication question each time, so it can't be memorized.

Privacy and your child's progress

Do I need to share my child's real name?

No. You do not need to share your child's real name when using OpenedCog. You are welcome to use your child's nickname or a name that they make up.

What information do you collect about my child?

None. OpenedCog has no accounts, analytics, cookies, ads, or third-party scripts, and every file it uses comes from its own site. Two things are outside our control, and we want you to know about them:

  • GitHub, which hosts the site, keeps standard logs that include visitors' IP addresses, for security.
  • Read-aloud uses your browser's built-in voices. Some browsers use online voices, which send the spoken puzzle text to the browser's maker. That text never includes anything about your child.
Where is my child's progress saved?

Only in the browser on the device that you use, and nowhere else. That's why your child's progress never leaves your device, and also why it can disappear.

Why did my child's progress disappear?

Usually one of these:

  • Browsing history or site data was cleared.
  • A private window was used.
  • You switched browsers or devices.
  • On an iPhone or iPad, Safari erased it after seven days of using Safari without visiting OpenedCog.

To prevent the last one, add OpenedCog to your Home Screen (Share button, then "Add to Home Screen"). To protect against all of them, save a backup file now and then from the Grown-ups page.

What's in a backup file, and how do I use it?

An OpenedCog backup is a small file containing each child's name or nickname, their practice results, and their settings. To make one, open Grown-ups and tap Save a backup file. To restore it on any device, tap Load a backup file, either on the welcome screen or on the Grown-ups page. Loading the same file twice won't duplicate anything. Keep the file private, since it holds your children's names and scores.

The real test and its scores

How will my child's CogAT scores be used?

That depends on your school district. Many districts use the CogAT to help decide eligibility for gifted or advanced programs, often alongside other information such as an achievement test, classroom work, or teacher input. Cutoffs and next steps vary widely. Your school can tell you how it uses the results.

Is the real test timed?

Not at Level 8. Riverside, the publisher, says these levels aren't timed. Its estimates are about 11 to 15 minutes per subtest, and many schools spread the nine subtests over three days. Your school sets the actual schedule.

My child gets everything right in practice. Should we do more?

No. That means the format is familiar, which is the goal. One pretend test day at 16 puzzles per part builds stamina. After that, a short session or two a week is enough until the test.

My child struggles with some puzzle types. Should we worry?

No. Every child finds some types harder, and these puzzles get harder toward the end of each set on purpose. Use learning mode and the coaching questions in the guide, try folding real paper, and keep sessions short and upbeat. Practice results don't predict the test.

Getting involved

I found a mistake in a puzzle. How do I report it?

Thank you! Please open an issue on GitHub with a screenshot and a sentence about what looks wrong.

How can I help?

The code and content are on GitHub. The code is MIT licensed, and the content is CC BY 4.0. Helpful contributions include reviewing or writing picture puzzles, testing on different devices, translating, and adapting OpenedCog for another level or language. Start by opening an issue to say what you'd like to do.

Can I use OpenedCog in my classroom or share it?

Yes. Share the link freely. You can also reuse or adapt the content under CC BY 4.0, as long as you credit "OpenedCog by Remi Kalir" and link back.
